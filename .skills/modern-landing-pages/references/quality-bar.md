# Modern Landing Page Quality Bar

## Strategy Checklist

- Audience: name the likely buyer or visitor, not a generic "user".
- Offer: make the page answer what it is, who it helps, and what to do next within the first viewport.
- Conversion: choose one primary action and one optional secondary action.
- Proof: include at least one credible proof type when content exists: logos, metrics, quote, review, case result, product screenshot, integrations, credentials, or guarantee.
- Objections: address risk, effort, price, security, setup time, switching cost, or trust before the final CTA.

## Hero Recipes

- Product UI hero: left-aligned concise copy, right or background product screenshot/mockup, small proof row, primary CTA.
- Editorial hero: full-bleed real/generative image with text overlay, brand or offer as the H1, CTA nearby, next section peeking below.
- Launch hero: announcement pill, literal product/offer H1, short outcome copy, product shot, waitlist/preorder CTA.
- Service hero: real service/person/place image, direct booking/contact CTA, trust microcopy, location or availability signal.

Avoid split layouts where both sides feel like cards. Avoid abstract gradient/SVG hero art when a product, place, person, or outcome can be shown.

## Section Recipes

- Proof strip: 4-8 logos or 2-4 crisp metrics, low contrast, compact height.
- Outcome blocks: 3 concise benefits, each tied to a user pain or measurable result.
- Product reveal: screenshot, annotated UI, workflow steps, before/after, or short interactive demo.
- Feature depth: group features by workflow or decision, not by internal system architecture.
- Social proof: quote with name/context, review cards, case-study snippets, or trust badges.
- Pricing: simple tiers with the most likely plan emphasized; include what happens after clicking.
- FAQ: 4-7 high-intent objections, not generic filler.
- Final CTA: restate the outcome, reduce risk, repeat the primary action.

## Visual Direction

- Combine neutral foundations with one or two purposeful accents. Do not let the page become only purple, blue-slate, beige, brown/orange, or any single hue family.
- Use strong spacing rhythm: generous hero spacing, tighter product/proof sections, compact cards.
- Use images with inspectable subject matter. Avoid dark, cropped, blurred, atmospheric stock visuals for pages where the thing being sold should be visible.
- Keep border radii moderate unless the existing design system says otherwise.
- Use shadows sparingly and consistently. Prefer borders, subtle contrast, and layout clarity over heavy glow effects.
- Make mobile layouts intentional: stacked content, full-width CTAs when useful, readable images, no tiny logo grids or crammed cards.

## Copy Patterns

- H1: product/category/offer plus direct outcome when concise.
- Subhead: who it helps, what changes, and why it is credible.
- CTA: verb plus action, short enough to fit on mobile.
- Benefits: start with outcomes, then support with features.
- Metrics: include context, not naked numbers.
- FAQ: answer directly in one short paragraph.

## Visual QA Pass

Check common viewports such as 390x844, 768x1024, 1440x900, and one wide desktop size when tooling allows.

- No overlapping text, clipped headings, hidden CTA labels, broken images, or horizontal scroll.
- Hero fits the viewport while leaving a visible cue of the next section.
- Images load, are not distorted, and have stable aspect ratios.
- Cards and buttons keep consistent dimensions across hover/focus states.
- Contrast is readable in hero overlays, muted text, badges, and disabled states.
- Navbar, forms, CTAs, and footer links are reachable and usable with keyboard focus.
- The page still makes sense with realistic long copy, not just ideal placeholder text.
